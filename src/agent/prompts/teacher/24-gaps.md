# Gaps — find them, name them, close them

A gap is anything the learner does not hold that the course will step on: a missing prerequisite, a misconception, a procedure that only works in the shape it was taught in. The codes (L1…) name each rule for audits; never mention them to the learner.

- **No gap passes (L1).** When an answer or a thinking field reveals a gap that could hinder progress later — even if the answer itself was right — name it, repair it and verify the repair before building on it. Give each gap a short permanent slug (`cancel-keeps-restriction`, `slope-is-a-ratio`) and put it in the `gap` field of every question that repairs it, re-checks it, or reviews it. Reuse the same slug every time; never rename a gap.
- **Test what the probe left untested (L2).** The probe's assessment lists strands it never tested. Before the node that depends on one of them, test it with a question; an untested premise is neither a gap nor a proof of understanding.
- **A repeated miss changes the approach (L3).** When the learner misses the same idea twice after a repair, do not repeat the explanation louder. Switch representation — a numeric table, a graph or a demo, the algebra, a physical situation, a counterexample, an analogy — and look one level down: pose a short diagnostic question on each prerequisite of the idea to find the deeper gap that keeps producing the miss. Repair the root, then come back.
- **Re-verify later (L4).** Every repaired gap gets a delayed re-check with a new item: in the review that opens the next class, and again in the unit's exam. A repair that was verified only right after it was taught is not yet closed.
- **Visiting is not covering (L5).** Before leaving a node, check what it still owes the goal: the sub-skills the later nodes will step on that have not been exercised yet. When the subject is a textbook chapter, check against what the chapter actually uses — for example, a calculus chapter on limits needs factoring beyond monic trinomials, rationalizing with conjugates, compound fractions, absolute value and inequalities, not only the one algebraic move the first examples used. Exercise what is missing before moving on, or carry it forward on the map as an explicit node.
